Specialization Pathways

Choosing a development pathway

The right timeline depends on how ready the Specialization and Courses are when the project begins.

This page is a planning guide, not a formal scope of work. The CTL Digital Program Development Team will confirm your project's specific timeline and support needs.

Shared assumptions

The Ready to publish, Ready to adapt, and Ready to develop pathways assume:

  • The Specialization uses standard Course and assessment formats.
  • Faculty have an active role in developing and reviewing the content.
  • Faculty prepare scripts, slides, readings, activities, assessments, and other source materials.
  • Slides and other visual materials are accessible and copyright-ready or can be revised within the project.
  • Copyright and accessibility questions can be resolved within the project.
  • Courses do not require complex tools, activities, integrations, or visual design.
  • There are no significant external requirements or multiple levels of review and approval.
  • The Courses can be developed and reviewed in a coordinated sequence.

These timelines are planning ranges rather than fixed commitments. More Courses, longer Courses, complex production needs, or additional review requirements may require more time.

Approximately 1–2 months

Ready to Publish

This pathway is a good fit if:

  • The Courses and Specialization content are complete and in good condition.
  • The Courses already have clear relationships and a coherent learner journey.
  • Course landing-page and Specialization landing-page content is ready or nearly ready.
  • Scripts, slides, readings, activities, assessments, and projects are ready to use.
  • Copyright, permissions, and ownership are clear.
  • Materials meet accessibility expectations or need only minor revisions.
  • The Specialization does not require substantial redesign or new content development.

The main work is reviewing, building, QA, and preparing the Courses and Specialization for publication.

Months 1–2: Review, build, and publish

You'll share your Course and Specialization content with the Learning Experience team. We'll review the materials, flag any copyright, accessibility, or learner-experience concerns, and identify any final revisions needed before the build. You'll address requested changes while the Learning Experience team builds and reviews the Courses and Specialization in Coursera. Courses and Specializations typically go live approximately seven days after submission, depending on the platform review process.

By the end of this stage, you'll have

  • Courses and Specialization content reviewed and ready for publication
  • Copyright and accessibility concerns addressed
  • Courses and Specialization built and reviewed in Coursera
  • Courses and Specialization submitted for publication

After launch

The Learning Experience team will monitor initial learner feedback, share relevant notes with you, and respond to learner support needs and technical issues throughout the life of the Courses and Specialization.

Approximately 4–6 months

Ready to Adapt

This pathway is a good fit if:

  • The Specialization includes approximately three to six Courses.
  • Most Course content already exists.
  • The Courses have a clear purpose and reasonably defined learning outcomes.
  • Existing materials are in good condition but need reframing for a commercial learning platform.
  • The Courses have standard structures and do not require complex production.
  • Faculty can prepare or revise scripts, slides, readings, activities, assessments, and projects.
  • Copyright and accessibility issues are limited and manageable.
  • Faculty can make coordinated decisions across the Courses.

The main work is adapting existing content, clarifying the learner journey, filling content gaps, resolving copyright and accessibility needs, building the Courses, and preparing the Specialization for launch. For planning purposes, expect approximately one month per Course, with additional time for a larger Specialization or more complex content.

Approximately 6–10 months

Ready to Develop

This pathway is a good fit if:

  • The Specialization includes approximately three to six Courses.
  • You have a clear vision for the overall learner experience.
  • The intended audience and Specialization-level outcomes are defined.
  • Each Course has a clear purpose or learning plan.
  • Some existing resources may be available, but most Learning Assets still need to be developed.
  • Faculty are comfortable preparing scripts, slides, readings, activities, assessments, and projects.
  • The Courses use standard formats and do not require complex production.
  • Faculty can make coordinated decisions and review content across the Courses.
  • There are no significant external requirements or multiple levels of review and approval.

The main work is developing the mapped videos, readings, activities, assessments, and projects across the Courses. A three-Course Specialization with clearly defined outcomes may fit toward the shorter end of this range. Additional Courses, longer Courses, or more involved assets will require more time.

Custom Development

A Specialization may require a custom timeline when the standard pathway assumptions do not apply. This pathway is a good fit if:

  • The Specialization needs continuing education or another formal review process.
  • The Courses must meet grant, accreditation, or other external requirements.
  • The project requires multiple levels of stakeholder review or approval.
  • The Specialization begins with an idea that needs substantial development.
  • The project requires significant pedagogical or curriculum support.
  • The Courses include complex videos, activities, tools, integrations, or visual design.
  • Faculty need substantial support developing scripts, slides, or other source materials.
  • The Specialization includes substantial new content development across multiple Courses.
  • The Courses are longer or substantially larger than the typical Specialization model.
  • The project includes co-design, consultation, research, or other activities that affect the development process.

Custom Specializations may include additional planning, consultation, co-design, media production, visual design, accessibility review, stakeholder review, continuing education review, or platform testing.

A tailored process — the Learning Experience team will work with you to define

  • The Specialization's purpose, audience, and scope
  • The role and main outcome of each Course
  • The learner journey across the Courses
  • The content and media production plan
  • The level of design and development support needed
  • The review, approval, and sign-off process
  • The timeline and major milestones
  • The roles and responsibilities of everyone involved

You may use the Specialization Design Guide, Course Design Guide, Module Design Guide, and Learning Assets Guide, but the team will identify which sections and resources are most relevant to the project. The timeline will be developed around the Specialization's specific needs — a three-Course or larger Custom Specialization will often require approximately 12–24 months.
