Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about choosing and adjusting a pathway.

The best fit depends on what you already have, how clearly the learning experience is defined, and how much development support the project will need.

Consider:

  • Is the content complete, mostly complete, or still being developed?
  • Are the learning objectives and Course or Specialization structure clear?
  • Are you comfortable preparing scripts, slides, activities, and other materials?
  • Will the project require substantial design, production, review, or approval?
  • Are there external requirements that may affect the timeline?

The CTL Digital Program Development Team can help you identify the most appropriate pathway.

That is common. The pathways are planning guides, not rigid categories.

For example, you may have existing content but still need substantial work to reorganize it into a coherent learning experience. Or you may have a strong learning plan but need more support developing the content than an accelerated timeline allows.

The CTL Digital Program Development Team can help you identify the closest fit and adjust the timeline based on the actual scope of work.

Existing content may include:

  • Videos or recorded lectures
  • Presentation slides
  • Readings or published materials
  • Activities or assignments
  • Quiz questions or assessments
  • Cases, examples, datasets, or other resources
  • Materials from an existing course, workshop, program, or project

Existing content does not necessarily mean content is ready to use. It may still need to be reorganized, shortened, rewritten, updated, made accessible, or reviewed for copyright and permissions.

A learning plan describes how learners will move through the experience, even if the individual materials have not yet been created.

A strong learning plan usually includes:

  • A clear audience and purpose
  • Defined learning objectives
  • A Course or Specialization structure
  • A sense of what each Module should help learners do
  • An idea of what learners will watch, read, practice, or complete
  • A reasonable understanding of what content still needs to be developed

You do not need to have every script or slide finished. The more clearly the learning experience is mapped, the more quickly development can begin.

Not necessarily. Existing content can shorten development when it is well organized, current, appropriate for the intended audience, and ready to adapt.

A large amount of content may still require significant time if it needs to be:

  • Reorganized
  • Condensed
  • Updated
  • Rewritten for an online audience
  • Converted into activities or assessments
  • Reviewed for accessibility or copyright
  • Aligned with new learning objectives

The condition and usability of the content matter more than the amount of content available.

That does not automatically mean the project needs a Custom timeline. A Course may still fit the Standard development pathway if the scope is manageable and the Course uses standard formats.

The main difference is that more time will be needed to:

  • Clarify the learning objectives
  • Plan the Modules
  • Identify the Learning Assets
  • Develop scripts, slides, activities, readings, and assessments
  • Test the development process with a demonstration Module

A clear outline or learning plan may support an Accelerated pathway if:

  • The learning objectives are well defined.
  • The Module structure is clear.
  • You are comfortable developing the content.
  • The Course uses standard formats.
  • You can make decisions and respond to feedback quickly.

If the outline is still broad or needs substantial instructional design development, the Standard pathway may be a better fit.

Faculty involvement varies by pathway, but every project requires active faculty participation.

You should expect to contribute time to:

  • Planning and design conversations
  • Preparing or reviewing content
  • Writing or revising scripts
  • Preparing slides and other source materials
  • Reviewing feedback
  • Making content decisions
  • Reviewing and approving the final Course

Accelerated timelines require more content to be ready and decisions to be made quickly. A longer timeline does not mean faculty contribute less; it allows more time for planning, development, revision, and review.

Possibly, but only when the project is ready and the team has enough capacity to support it.

Shorter timelines are more realistic when:

  • Content is complete or nearly complete.
  • Learning objectives and structure are clear.
  • Faculty can prepare materials quickly.
  • The Course uses standard formats.
  • Copyright and accessibility questions are limited.
  • There are few reviewers and decision-makers.
  • Feedback and approvals happen on schedule.

Compressing the timeline without reducing the work usually creates bottlenecks later in development.

A project may need additional time when it includes:

  • A longer Course or Specialization
  • More Modules or Learning Assets
  • Substantial new content development
  • Complex activities, tools, or integrations
  • Specialized media or visual design
  • Continuing education or accreditation review
  • Grant or stakeholder requirements
  • Multiple rounds of review or approval
  • Co-design, consultation, or research activities
  • Delays in faculty availability, feedback, or decision-making

The CTL Digital Program Development Team can help identify these factors early.

For planning purposes, a standard Course typically includes:

  • 2–4 Course learning objectives
  • 3–6 Modules
  • 2–8 hours of learner time
  • Standard formats such as videos, readings, activities, and auto-graded assessments

A Course outside this range may still be appropriate for a standard pathway, but the scope and timeline may need to be adjusted.

For planning purposes, a standard Specialization typically includes:

  • 3–6 Courses
  • 8–48 hours of total learner time
  • One or more applied projects per Course
  • A clear relationship among the Courses
  • Standard Course and assessment formats

A larger or more complex Specialization may require additional time or a Custom development process.

A project may need a Custom timeline when its needs extend beyond the assumptions of the standard pathways.

This may include projects with:

  • Complex learning experiences or production needs
  • Substantial pedagogical development
  • External review or approval requirements
  • Grant, accreditation, or continuing education criteria
  • Multiple stakeholder groups
  • Significant co-design or consultation
  • New tools, integrations, or interactive formats
  • A large amount of new content
  • A scope that is substantially larger than the typical Course or Specialization

Custom does not mean the project is unsuitable. It means the project needs a more tailored process and timeline.

The pathway provides a starting estimate. The project team will confirm the timeline after reviewing:

  • The project goals and scope
  • Existing content and materials
  • Learning objectives and structure
  • Faculty availability
  • Development and production needs
  • Review and approval requirements

The final project timeline will reflect the specific Course or Specialization rather than the pathway label alone.
